How to Hand a WordPress Site to a Client Without Them Breaking the Layout

Oct 29, 2026 Kerf Team 5 min read

Getting a design onto WordPress is one job. Handing the finished site to the person who will live in it is another, and it is where good builds quietly fall apart. The client wants to change a headline, so they open the editor, click around, and by Tuesday a column has been dragged out of its grid.

This is a short checklist for a handover that survives the first week, using the parts of Kerf that exist for exactly this.

What Goes Wrong at Handover

An imported design is real HTML and CSS: a CSS Grid with exactly the children it expects, a wrapper div the stylesheet depends on. In WordPress’s block editor, every one of those is a block that can be moved, deleted or replaced. Nobody does it on purpose. A client who only wanted to change a sentence can still drag a block, press Delete, or add a block where the layout has no room for one.

Client Mode: Lock the Structure, Leave the Content

Switch it on under Kerf → Settings. With Client mode on, blocks cannot be moved, inserted or deleted, and the raw HTML tag and class attributes are locked. Everything a client actually edits stays open:

LockedStill editable
Moving, adding and deleting blocksEvery heading and paragraph
Raw tag and class attributesButton labels
Images and background pictures
Link destinations

You choose who it applies to: Editor roles only, which leaves Administrators unrestricted, or everyone until you switch it off again. A common setup is to keep your own Administrator account unrestricted and give the client an Editor account.

Put the Business Details in One Place

Phone numbers, email addresses, street addresses and opening hours usually appear in the header, the footer and the contact page at once. Kerf finds them during the import (or you mark them with data-kerf-site="phone|email|address|hours|name") and links them to Kerf → Site details. Change the number once on that screen and every linked spot updates, including tel: and mailto: links. The client no longer has to hunt through three templates when the opening hours change. Let Links Follow the Pages

Kerf keeps your navigation markup as written and binds only each link’s destination to a WordPress page. If a client renames a page’s slug or moves it under a parent, the links to it update everywhere. Open Kerf → Links to see every internal link and any that do not point at a page yet. How the menu keeps your markup →

Check That the Contact Form Reaches Someone

A form that looks finished and sends nothing is the most embarrassing thing a client can discover. Kerf turns on Send this form with Kerf for imported forms: the message is emailed to the site’s recipient address and also saved under Kerf → Form entries. Email delivery still depends on the host, so send a test message before you hand over. The contact form guide →

A Handover Checklist

  1. Open Kerf → Links and point or create anything left unmatched.
  2. Fill in Kerf → Site details so the phone, email, address and hours are right once.
  3. Submit the contact form yourself and confirm the email arrives and the message is in Form entries.
  4. Turn on Client mode for Editor roles, then create the client’s Editor account.
  5. Keep the ZIP you imported. When the design changes later, Update design applies it section by section without wiping your client’s edits.
